# Getting Started with TagTracker

Welcome. This is a quick orientation to get you through your first
shift — not a manual. For anything beyond this, type `help` at the
prompt, or ask a colleague.

## What it's for

Bike parking here works like a coat check: each bike gets a two-piece
tag, one half on the bike, one half to the owner. TagTracker is just the
program that keeps track of which tag is currently attached to a bike,
so when someone comes back with their half, you know which bike is
theirs. That's it — the whole job is remembering that.

## How you talk to it

You'll be sitting at a simple text prompt. Mostly, you just type the
tag's letters and numbers and press enter — TagTracker figures out
whether that means the bike is arriving or leaving, based on what it
already knows about that tag today. You'll rarely need to type a full
word.

If it ever guesses wrong, or you need to be specific, there are `in`
and `out` commands for that — `help` will show you the exact wording
whenever you need it. You don't need to memorize anything before your
first shift; the prompt will tell you what it needs.

## It's hard to break

Everything you enter is saved immediately — there's no "save" step, and
no risk of losing a day's work to a crash or a restart. If you type
something wrong, there are ways to fix it (edit, delete, and a quick
`undo` for same-minute typos), so don't be afraid to just try things.
Worst case, ask — nothing you do at this prompt is going to cause real
harm.

## A few things you'll pick up fast

- Each day starts fresh. What happened yesterday doesn't carry over.
- Occasionally a tag won't be available even though nothing seems to be
  on it — it might be retired (permanently out of service) or held
  (set aside just for today, usually a leftover-from-yesterday or
  set-aside-mid-day bike). TagTracker will tell you which, if you ask
  it about that tag.
- There are report commands (`tags`, `audit`, and others) that show you
  the state of things at a glance. You'll be shown these as you need
  them.

## Where to go next

- `help` — the full list of commands, and `help <command>` for details
